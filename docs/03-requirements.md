# PhD Computer Science

### Responsible Conduct of Research

All entering graduate students take a workshop on the Responsible Conduct of Research (RCR) offered by the Office of Research and Sponsored Programs at the Graduate Center.   The RCR workshop covers plagiarism, authorship attribution on publications, advisor-trainee relationships and the control of data.  This mandatory workshop is offered in the Fall and the Spring semesters.  All students must take this workshop in the first year of their graduate studies at CUNY. 


### Level II

For advancement to Level II, a student must have:

* Cleared the first exam
* Taken 45 credits of course work, possibly including up to 30 credits transferred in from a previous Master's degree.
* Completed the course CSC 80010 Research Survey.

## Second Exam and Advancement to Level III

A student's time in Level II is meant to achieve two things: 

1. Find a field and a mentor, if not yet chosen.
2. Finish up the coursework part of the PhD studies.

Readiness to start research in a chosen field of interest is proven through the Second Exam. For the Second Exam, a student will:

1. Select a Second Exam Committee, which must include your advisor and at least two members of the doctoral faculty in Computer Science or doctoral faculty at the Graduate Center in a related interdisciplinary field.
2. Write a survey of your chosen research field. This written report is meant to be a base for a Background chapter in a doctoral dissertation, and must be distributed to the committee and to the Executive Officer **at least four weeks** prior to the Second Exam Presentation.
3. Prepare and hold a presentation of the second exam. This is an open seminar followed by a closed questioning with the committee.

### Research Tool

Before advancing to candidacy (Level III), a student is required to show high-level programming proficiency by submitting to the Executive Officer a **large computer program, written by themselves**. This program can be one written in industry, one developed on his/her own, or one developed as part of a course that requires the writing of a large program.

The research tool **must** be a program that:

* was written solely by the potential candidate;
* is of sufficient size and complexity to demonstrate the candidate’s programming proficiency;
* is of professional quality in terms of modularity, functionality, and program validity;
* is well-documented with in-code comments written in English and README/instructional documents that make it easy to utilize, and;
* is published in an online software repository such as GitHub.

The research tool should be sent to both the Executive Officer and the CS office (compsci@gc.cuny.edu) as a link to a public GitHub repository. Written prior approval is required from the EO if the tool cannot be shared publicly. The research tool must be sent to the EO and CS office at least two weeks prior to any level change deadlines; it is the student's responsibility to consult the GC Academic Calendar for said deadlines.


### Level III

Once the student has

* Passed the Second Exam
* Completed 60 credits, at least 30 of those in residence (may include the 80200 Computer Science Research course up to twice)
* No remaining incomplete grades and a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.00 (B)
* Shown an acceptable research tool.

The student can then **advance to Level III status**


## Dissertation Proposal

During Level III, the student must write and defend a Dissertation Proposal that delineates an intended dissertation research topic. This proposal must convince their committee that the topic is worthy of doctoral research, both for originality and significance, and that they have an approach to handling the topic that shows they are conversant with the state of the art directly relevant to their proposed research and can be done in a reasonable amount of time.

The student must:

1. Select a Dissertation Proposal Committee. This Committee usually extends the Second Exam Committee and must consist of at least three members of the doctoral faculty at the Graduate Center, including your advisor, as well as one outside member - external to CUNY.
2. Write and submit a Dissertation Proposal. This document must be distributed to the committee and the Executive Officer **at least four weeks** prior to the oral presentation.
3. Hold an oral presentation and examination. This is a 45 minute open seminar followed by a closed questioning by the committee. 

```{note}
If a student changes area of intended research, the Second Exam Committee may determine that a new Second Exam is needed before the Dissertation Proposal defense can be scheduled.
```

## Advancement to Candidacy

After a successful proposal defense, the time is to do the planned research and write the dissertation. 

## Dissertation Defense and Submission

To complete PhD requirements, students **must**:

1. Select an Examining Committee.
2. Submit their work for publication in peer-reviewed conferences or professional journals, by the time of defense. (This is required for students who start in the program after Fall 2022, and suggested to all other current students.)
3. Write a clear, original, and significant dissertation.
4. Orally present a dissertation defense to the Examining Committee, who subsequently approves the presentation and report.
5. Deposit their dissertation. 

The Examining Committee has to include your advisor, at least two other members of the doctoral faculty in Computer Science or a GC doctoral faculty member in a related interdisciplinary field, and an outside member who is required to participate in the Final Examination.

Students should be in close contact with their mentors and members of their Examining Committees throughout the "dissertation process," sending them drafts and soliciting their comments. When a final draft of the dissertation is completed and deemed acceptable by the Examining Committee, the student should schedule a mutually acceptable date and time with the members of his or her Examining Committee and the Executive Officer – when all will be able to attend the presentation and defense. The exam should be scheduled for three hours.

Students are required to email a draft of their dissertation in the Program Office and to all members of their committee at least four weeks prior to the time of the Final Examination. Along with this draft, they must provide the names and contact information (i.e., email addresses) of their Examining Committee members.

### Deposit your Dissertation

Once successfully defended, the student must deposit the approved final version of the dissertation at the Mina Rees Library. Regulations concerning the style of the dissertation and deposit procedures are available at the Office of the Dissertation Research Librarian, located in Room 2304 (2nd floor) in the Mina Rees Library (212-817-7069). You may also wish to view the Mina Rees Library [thesis and dissertation guide page](http://libguides.gc.cuny.edu/content.php?pid=231531) for details.

## Advisor Selection

A student who is funded on a Tuition Fellowship or a Half Science Fellowship will be drawing on support from a faculty member's funding sources from the start. These students are expected to work with the faculty member who is supporting them.

A student on a GCF, or studying while already employed at CUNY, is at liberty to choose faculty mentor more freely. These students should use their time in Level I and Level II to explore the faculty available in the program, the ongoing research, and settle on an interesting direction to take their studies.

## En Route Master's Degree

The Graduate Center awards an en route M. Phil. Degree to students who advance to Ph.D. candidacy as described above.  If a student decides to withdraw from the Program prior to advancing to Ph.D. candidacy, they may request a master’s degree from the CUNY college at which their research was performed.  These master’s degree may be M.A. or M.S. degrees.  Students should consult with the campus Department Chair at their campus as to the precise requirements for a campus-based master’s degree.  In general, the requirements are, as follows:

1. a minimum of 45 credits with an average grade of B
2. passing the First Examination, and
3. satisfactory completion of a major research paper

The requirement of 45 credits cannot include courses for which SP grades are received or any advanced standing transfer credits.  The student who wishes to receive a campus-based master's degree should make an appointment with the Executive Officer and the campus Department Chair.

# MSc Data Science

For a MSc Data Science, the student must complete:

1. The four MSc Core Courses.
2. Four freely chosen electives courses. (a few courses specific to the PhD program are not available as electives)
3. A capstone internship or two capstone projects.

Finding an internship or a faculty advisor for the capstone projects is the responsibility of the student.

# Advanced Certificate Data Science

For an Advanced Certificate in Data Sciecne, the student must complete the four MSc Core Courses.

Students not in the Computer Scence or Data Science programs who want to pursue the advanced certificate should contact the program office about evaluating their readiness for the courses before receiving an exceptional allowance to enroll in the core courses.
